---
- **Where**: Analyze › Forecast works on the open tab's last result. Pick **Date** (a date or time column; dates kept as text work too), **Value** (a number, or Count of rows), **Per step** (sum, avg, count, min, max), **Step** (Pick for me, hour, day, week, month, quarter, year), **Steps ahead** (empty = 24 hours, 14 days, 8 weeks, 6 months, 4 quarters, 3 years; at most 120) and **One per** (a category column: one forecast per group, the 12 largest). Then Forecast. You run the same with `forecast`.
- **Pick for me** chooses the step from the span of the dates: hours under 4 days, days under 2 years, weeks under 3 years, months beyond.
- **How it works**: the server sums the result into one value per step and fills steps with no rows (0 for a sum or count, the line between neighbours for avg / min / max). A first or last step that holds fewer dates than usual (data that stops mid-week) is left out, with a warning. Four models then compete on the last part of the history, held back in up to three rounds: last value again, same as one season ago, smoothing with a damped trend (Holt), and Holt-Winters with a repeating pattern (weekly for days, daily for hours, yearly for weeks, months and quarters). The model with the smallest error there is fitted on the whole series. At most the last 1,000 steps are used.
- **What it shows**: the next step with its likely range (80%), the total over the horizon (or the last value for avg / min / max), the accuracy against repeating the past (+23% = 23% smaller error), the model chosen, a chart (history grey, forecast dashed in its 80% and 95% bands, unusual steps as red dots), a sentence, **Read with care** warnings, the **Unusual** steps (far from the same phase in the seasons around them), the per-group table ranked by expected change, and the forecast in numbers.
- **How to read it**: tell the user the range, not only the line, and repeat the warnings. A forecast assumes the past pattern continues; it cannot know about a promotion, a price change or a holiday it has not seen. Less than two full cycles of history means no repeating pattern can be learned. When the result says repeating the past did as well as any model, say so plainly.
- **Dashboards**: the Forecast widget (Add to dashboard › Forecast, or `build_dashboard` kind `forecast`) takes the date as X and the number as Y (empty = row count), with Using as the per-step aggregate, and draws the forecast with its bands. It refreshes when the query runs again. Build from this result adds one when the result has a date with at least 8 different values (a forecast needs 8 steps).
- Never the rows: `forecast` returns the forecast, its accuracy, warnings, unusual steps and groups, not the history.
